Words to Know

Full package
A 100% tuition waiver plus a monthly stipend for living costs.
Partly funded
A scholarship that pays only part of your costs.
Tuition waiver
The school does not charge you tuition, or charges only part of it. Also called a tuition exemption.
Stipend
Money paid to you every month for rent, food and transport. Also called a living allowance.
Competitive
A committee chooses who gets the money. Meeting the rules is not enough.
Separate application
A second application only for the scholarship, apart from the admission application.
GAP
Graduate Assistantship Programme (NUS and NTU). Paid teaching or research assistant hours that come with the scholarship.
MOE / MOFA Taiwan Scholarship
Scholarships from Taiwan's Ministry of Education and Ministry of Foreign Affairs. You apply through the Taiwan office in your home country.
ICDF
Taiwan International Cooperation and Development Fund. Gives full scholarships to citizens of partner countries.
GKS / NIIED
Global Korea Scholarship, run by Korea's National Institute for International Education.
BGS / CSCSE
Beijing Government Scholarship. CSCSE is the Chinese office that verifies degrees from outside China.
Erasmus Mundus
A joint master's program run by several European universities. EU scholarships pay tuition and a stipend.
ECTS
European credit points. One year of full-time study is 60 ECTS, so a 2-year master's is 120.
CEFR (B1, B2, C1)
The European scale of language levels. B1 is intermediate, C1 is advanced.
EMI certificate
An official letter from your university saying your degree was taught in English. Some programs accept it instead of IELTS or TOEFL.
Transcript
The official list of your courses and grades.
Expected-graduation certificate
A letter from your university saying when you will graduate. Use it if you apply before you have your diploma.
Statement of Purpose / Letter of Intent
An essay about why you want the program and what you will do after it.
Study plan / Research proposal
A short plan for what you want to study or research, with your question and methods.
Referee report
Another name for a recommendation letter.
Notarized translation
A translation that a notary confirms is correct.
Apostille / Consular legalization
Official stamps that prove a document is real in another country. Taiwan is not part of the Apostille Convention, so Taiwanese documents usually need legalization instead.
Proof of funds
A bank statement that shows you can pay your living costs. Taiwan programs ask for NT$ 120,000 to 200,000.

Timeline for 2027 entry

  1. November 2026

    NUS closes 1 Nov. NTU closes 15 Nov. STUST (spring 2027 intake) closes 30 Nov. Book IELTS or TOEFL and ask for recommendation letters.

  2. December 2026

    NTUT and the ICDF scholarship open 1 Dec. PKU closes 25 Dec.

  3. January 2027

    Aarhus (Mundus Journalism) closes 10 Jan. Gothenburg and Lund close 15 Jan. Jyväskylä closes 21 Jan.

  4. February 2027

    Gothenburg fee and documents due 1 Feb for the Axel Adler Scholarship. MOE and MOFA Taiwan Scholarships and MCU (fall intake) open 1 Feb. Lund Global Scholarship opens in early February. CLIDE opens 22 Feb.

  5. March 2027

    CLIDE closes 7 Mar. NCCU Round 2 closes about 9 Mar. NTUT closes 10 Mar. ICDF closes 15 Mar. MOE and MOFA close 31 Mar.

  6. April to May 2027

    Results arrive (Axel Adler 12 Apr, NTUT early May). MCU closes 31 May. Accept one offer and decline the others.

  7. June to August 2027

    PKU scholarship window ends 15 Jun. Apply for your visa, find housing, and send your final diploma.

  8. August to September 2027

    Programs start: NTU 1 Aug, NUS 9 Aug, Gothenburg and Lund 30 Aug, Aarhus 1 Sep, and the Taiwan programs and PKU in September.

SNU and Yonsei: the rounds in our notes have closed (9 Jul 2026 and 20 Mar 2026). Check their websites for the next round.

Tips on translations and authentication

  • Ask for English first. IBSC is taught in English, so ask YZU for your diploma and transcript in English. Most programs then need no translation. Taiwan programs also accept Chinese.
  • Translations. NCCU, NUS, NTU, PKU, SNU, Yonsei and Aarhus want certified or notarized translations of any document that is not in English (or Chinese or Korean where accepted).
  • Korea (SNU, Yonsei). Both ask for an apostille or Korean consular legalization of your degree and transcript. Taiwan is not part of the Apostille Convention, so ask the school whether legalization through the Korean Mission in Taipei is the right route.
  • China (PKU). Degrees from outside Mainland China are verified by CSCSE.
  • Taiwan (MCU, STUST, NTUT). Foreign academic documents must be verified by a Taiwan office abroad. Your YZU documents are issued in Taiwan, so ask the admissions office whether this step applies to you.
  • Aarhus, Munich track. An APS certificate is only needed for degrees from China, India or Vietnam.
  • When a program says nothing, email admissions and ask: "Do you need notarized copies, an apostille, or legalization by an embassy?" Keep their answer.
