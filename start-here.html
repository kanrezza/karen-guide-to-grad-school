Words to Know

Tuition waiverThe school does not charge you tuition, or charges only part of it.
StipendMoney paid to you every month for rent, food, and transport.
Full packageA 100% tuition waiver plus a stipend that covers basic living costs in that city.
CompetitiveNot every admitted student gets the money. A committee chooses.
TA / RATeaching assistant / research assistant. A paid job at the university that can cover your costs.
TranscriptThe official list of your courses and grades.
GPAGrade point average. Your average grade, for example 3.5 out of 4.0.
Letter of expected graduationA letter from your university that says when you will graduate. Use it if you apply before you get your diploma.
Statement of Purpose / Personal statementAn essay about why you want the program and what you will do after it.
Research proposal / Study planA short plan for the research you want to do: your question, your methods, and your sources.
Recommendation letterA letter from a professor or employer about your skills.
NotarizationA notary confirms that a copy or a translation is true.
ApostilleA stamp that makes a document valid in other countries that signed the Hague Convention.
LegalizationAn embassy or representative office confirms that a document is real.
ECTSEuropean credit points. A two-year master's degree is 120 ECTS.
Erasmus MundusA master's program run by several European universities together and funded by the European Union.
MOE, MOFA, ICDFTaiwan's Ministry of Education, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and International Cooperation and Development Fund. All three give scholarships.
MEXT, CGS, SIGovernment scholarships of Japan, China, and Sweden.
GRE, GMATGraduate admission tests from the USA. Few programs in this guide ask for them.
TOCFL, JLPT, TOPIKChinese, Japanese, and Korean language tests.

General application checklist

  1. Check that your passport is valid for the whole program.
  2. Ask IBSC for an official transcript and a letter of expected graduation in English.
  3. Take IELTS or TOEFL, or ask IBSC for a letter that says your degree is taught in English.
  4. Ask two professors for recommendation letters at least six weeks before the deadline.
  5. Write your Statement of Purpose. Follow the word or page limit for each program.
  6. Write a research proposal if the program asks for one.
  7. Update your CV.
  8. Choose a writing sample, for example your IBSC graduation project or your best paper.
  9. Prepare a bank statement or proof of your scholarship.
  10. Pay the application fee.
  11. Send the separate scholarship application if the scholarship has one.
  12. Save a copy of everything you send and every email you get.

Tips on translations and authentication

  • IBSC teaches in English. Ask the registrar for your diploma and transcript in English first. This often means you do not need a translation.
  • NCCU (Taiwan): documents that are not in English or Chinese need a notarized translation.
  • MOE Taiwan Scholarship: documents that are not in English or Chinese must be translated and the translation authenticated by a Taiwan representative office abroad.
  • Communication University of China: documents that are not in Chinese or English need a notarized translation.
  • Mundus Journalism, Munich track: degrees from China, India, and Vietnam need an APS certificate. Start early, because it takes several weeks.
  • If a program does not say what it needs, email the admissions office and ask: "Do you need notarized copies, an apostille, or legalization by an embassy?" Keep their answer.
