Words to Know

Full package
100% tuition waiver plus a monthly stipend.
Partly funded
The scholarship pays only part of your costs.
Tuition waiver
The school does not charge some or all tuition.
Stipend
Monthly money for rent, food and transport.
Competitive
A committee chooses. Meeting the rules is not enough.
Separate application
A second form for the scholarship, apart from admission.
MOE / MOFA / ICDF
Taiwan government scholarships. You apply through the Taiwan office in your home country.
GKS, BGS, GSFS, GAP
Korean, Beijing, SNU and Singapore scholarship schemes.
Erasmus Mundus
A joint master's degree run by several European universities, with EU scholarships.
ECTS
European credits. 60 ECTS = one year of study.
CEFR (B1, B2, C1)
Language levels. B1 = intermediate, C1 = advanced.
EMI certificate
A letter from your university saying your degree is taught in English.
Transcript
The official list of your courses and grades.
Expected-graduation certificate
A letter that says when you will graduate.
Statement of Purpose
An essay on why you want the program and your goals.
Study plan / Research proposal
A short plan of what you will study or research.
Notarized translation
A translation that a notary confirms is correct.
Apostille / Legalization
Stamps that prove a document is real abroad. Taiwan uses legalization.
Proof of funds
A bank statement showing you can pay living costs.

Tips on translations and authentication

  • Ask YZU for English documents first. Then most programs need no translation.
  • Translate only documents that are not in English (or Chinese for Taiwan).
  • SNU and Yonsei ask for an apostille. Taiwan cannot issue one, so ask about legalization at the Korean Mission in Taipei.
  • PKU verifies foreign degrees through CSCSE.
  • If a program says nothing, email admissions and keep the reply.
